Does Pennsylvania Tax Retirement Income? What Retirees Should Know

Pennsylvania is one of the more retiree-friendly states for income tax: Social Security, pensions, and most retirement account withdrawals are generally exempt. The details, especially the age 59½ rule, matter for anyone planning an early retirement or a move.

The short answer

Generally no. Pennsylvania does not tax Social Security, and it generally does not tax pensions, 401(k)s, or IRA withdrawals once you are retired and past the plan’s retirement conditions or age 59½. It does tax interest, dividends, and capital gains at a flat 3.07%.

Social Security

Social Security benefits are not subject to Pennsylvania personal income tax, regardless of your income. That matters most for higher earners, because up to 85% of benefits can still be taxable on the federal return.

Pensions, 401(k)s, and other employer plans

Payments from an employer-sponsored retirement plan are generally exempt once you have met the plan’s conditions for retirement, such as its age or years-of-service requirement, and have actually retired. Payments taken before then, for example from a former employer’s plan while you are still working elsewhere, can be partly taxable. Pennsylvania taxed your contributions when you made them, so the taxable portion is generally limited to the amount above what you put in.

IRAs and the age 59½ rule

IRA withdrawals are generally not taxable in Pennsylvania once you reach 59½. Before that age, the part of a distribution that exceeds your contributions can be taxable, using a cost-recovery calculation. Two details catch people:

  • Pennsylvania does not follow the federal exceptions. A withdrawal that avoids the federal 10% penalty, such as substantially equal periodic payments, can still be taxable for Pennsylvania if you are under 59½.
  • Early retirees should plan the order of withdrawals. If you retire at 55, drawing first from taxable accounts or an employer plan that allows penalty-free access, and waiting on IRA withdrawals until 59½, can avoid Pennsylvania tax on those IRA dollars.

What Pennsylvania does tax

Interest, dividends, and capital gains are taxed at the flat 3.07% rate, with no lower rate for long-term gains. Pennsylvania also has no capital loss carryforward: a net loss in one year cannot offset gains in the next. For retirees living on a taxable portfolio, investment income is usually the main Pennsylvania tax. Our guide to tax drag explains how to measure and reduce it. For how Treasuries, municipal bonds, and corporates compare after Pennsylvania tax, see our bond investing guide.

Local earned income taxes apply to wages and self-employment income, not to retirement plan distributions or investment income.

Planning ideas for Pennsylvania retirees

Before 59½

Sequence early withdrawals

Fund the years before 59½ from taxable savings or an eligible employer plan rather than IRAs to avoid Pennsylvania tax on early IRA withdrawals.

Federal brackets

Roth conversions

Pennsylvania generally does not tax IRA distributions after 59½, which keeps the focus of a conversion plan on federal brackets and Medicare thresholds. See our Roth conversion guide.

Taxable accounts

Manage investment income

Because only investment income is taxed, asset location and tax-efficient funds reduce what is left of your Pennsylvania bill.

Legacy

Plan for inheritance tax

IRAs left to children are generally subject to the 4.5% inheritance tax and later to federal income tax. Beneficiary choices and lifetime conversions can change that.

Frequently asked questions

Does Pennsylvania tax retirement income?

Generally no. Pennsylvania does not tax Social Security benefits, and it generally does not tax pension, 401(k), 403(b), or 457 payments received after you meet your plan’s retirement conditions and retire, or IRA distributions taken after age 59½. Pennsylvania does tax interest, dividends, and capital gains at its flat 3.07% rate.

Does Pennsylvania tax Social Security?

No. Social Security benefits are not subject to Pennsylvania personal income tax.

Does Pennsylvania tax IRA withdrawals?

IRA distributions are generally not taxable in Pennsylvania once you reach age 59½. Distributions taken before 59½ can be taxable to the extent they exceed your contributions, because Pennsylvania taxed those contributions when they were made. Pennsylvania does not follow the federal exceptions to the early-withdrawal penalty.

Is Pennsylvania a tax-friendly state for retirees?

For most retirees, yes. Pennsylvania exempts Social Security and most retirement plan income and taxes other income at a flat 3.07%. The main trade-offs are property taxes, which vary widely by school district, and Pennsylvania’s inheritance tax, which applies to most transfers to children and other heirs other than a spouse.
