Qualified Charitable Distributions: The Most Tax-Efficient Way to Give After 70½

If you are 70½ or older and give to charity, sending the gift straight from your IRA usually beats writing a check. The money never shows up in your taxable income, and it can count toward your required minimum distribution. Here is how it works in 2026.

How a QCD works

A qualified charitable distribution is a transfer from your IRA directly to a qualifying charity. Because the money goes straight from the IRA custodian to the charity, it is excluded from your income entirely. You don’t also take a charitable deduction for it; the exclusion is the benefit.

That exclusion is usually worth more than a deduction. Most retirees take the standard deduction and get little or no tax benefit from writing a check. A QCD lowers your adjusted gross income itself, which can also reduce the taxable portion of Social Security, Medicare premium surcharges, and exposure to the Net Investment Income Tax.

A worked example

A married couple in their mid-70s takes the standard deduction. The husband’s required minimum distribution this year is $40,000, and they give $15,000 a year to their church and local charities.

Who qualifies, and which accounts

  • Age 70½ on the day of the distribution. That is earlier than the age when required minimum distributions begin, so QCDs can start before RMDs do.
  • Traditional IRAs and inherited IRAs. SEP and SIMPLE IRAs qualify only if no employer contribution is being made that year. 401(k) and 403(b) plans don’t qualify; roll the money to an IRA first.
  • Each spouse has their own limit, from their own IRA.
  • Public charities only. Gifts to donor-advised funds, supporting organizations, and most private foundations do not qualify.

Why QCDs matter more in 2026

Starting in 2026, people who itemize deduct charitable gifts only above 0.5% of their AGI, and the top bracket’s benefit is capped at 35%. Non-itemizers gain a new deduction of up to $1,000 ($2,000 for couples) for cash gifts. A QCD sidesteps all of these limits because the gift never enters your income in the first place.

No extra benefit in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania generally doesn’t tax IRA withdrawals after age 59½, so a QCD saves federal tax, not Pennsylvania tax. See how Pennsylvania taxes retirement income.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Taking the RMD first. Withdrawals count toward your RMD in the order they happen. Do the QCD before taking the rest of your distribution, not after.
  • Waiting until late December. The money must leave the IRA by December 31, and custodians often set earlier cutoffs. Checks mailed to charities can also take time to clear.
  • Getting something in return. Gala tickets or other benefits disqualify the gift. Get a written acknowledgment from each charity.
  • Missing it at tax time. Your Form 1099-R won’t identify the QCD. Report the excluded amount on your return and write “QCD” next to it, or tell your preparer.
  • Deductible IRA contributions after 70½. Those reduce how much of your QCDs can be excluded.

Frequently asked questions

What is a qualified charitable distribution?

A qualified charitable distribution (QCD) is a transfer of up to $111,000 in 2026 from an IRA directly to a qualifying charity by an IRA owner age 70½ or older. The amount is excluded from taxable income and can count toward the owner’s required minimum distribution.

What is the QCD limit for 2026?

The QCD limit is $111,000 per IRA owner for 2026, and a married couple can each give up to that amount from their own IRAs. There is also a one-time election to give up to $55,000 through a QCD to a charitable remainder trust or charitable gift annuity.

Can a QCD go to a donor-advised fund?

No. QCDs cannot be made to donor-advised funds, supporting organizations, or most private foundations. They must go directly to an eligible public charity.

Does a QCD count toward my required minimum distribution?

Yes. A QCD counts toward your required minimum distribution for the year. Distributions count in the order they are taken, so make the QCD before withdrawing the rest of your RMD if you want it to reduce the taxable amount.

How do I report a QCD on my tax return?

Your IRA custodian reports the distribution on Form 1099-R without identifying it as a QCD. On Form 1040, report the full distribution, enter the taxable amount net of the QCD, and write “QCD” next to the line. Keep the charity’s written acknowledgment.

Year-end QCD checklist

Before December 31

  1. Confirm you are 70½ on the date of the gift.
  2. Check your custodian’s QCD deadline.
  3. Do the QCD before taking the rest of your RMD.
  4. Confirm each charity is a public charity, not a DAF.
  5. Get written acknowledgments with no benefits received.
  6. Tell your tax preparer the total you gave by QCD.
