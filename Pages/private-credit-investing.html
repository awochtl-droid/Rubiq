Beyond the headline yield

Private credit is an investment decision—not a stand-alone product choice.

Private credit generally refers to loans and other debt investments made outside public bond markets, often through private funds. Direct lending, real estate debt, and specialty finance can have very different borrowers, structures, fees, and risks.

A headline distribution rate does not tell you how a strategy may behave in a stressed market, when capital can be returned, or how it fits alongside your other illiquid investments. Any review should begin with the purpose of the allocation and the household's capacity to take risk and wait for liquidity.

What an investment review covers

Look through the fund to the risks underneath.

For a first allocation to private credit or a review of an existing holding, evaluate the investment as part of the whole portfolio—not in isolation.

01 · Strategy

What is being financed?

Understand the borrowers, loan purpose, collateral, seniority, diversification, and the conditions that could lead to default or recovery.

02 · Manager

How are loans sourced?

Review underwriting standards, sourcing, monitoring, workout capabilities, experience across cycles, and alignment with investors.

03 · Terms

When is capital available?

Read the fee structure, leverage, fund term, redemption limits, transfer rules, valuation process, and any capital-call obligations.

04 · Portfolio

Does it fit the plan?

Consider concentration, existing private-market exposure, cash needs, taxes, and how the investment affects portfolio risk and flexibility.

Risks to understand

Potential income does not remove investment risk.

Private credit can involve meaningful uncertainty. The investment's legal documents and offering materials—not a target distribution or summary—define its rights, obligations, and risks.

  • Credit and default risk: a borrower may fail to pay, and recovery may be partial or delayed.
  • Illiquidity: transfers and redemptions may be restricted; capital may be committed for an extended period.
  • Valuation uncertainty: private holdings may not have frequent market prices, so reported values can differ from realizable value.
  • Leverage and concentration: borrowing and exposure to a limited set of borrowers or sectors can magnify losses.
  • Distributions are uncertain: income and return of capital may vary, be delayed, or fall below expectations.

Common questions

Private credit investing FAQs

What is private credit investing?

Private credit generally refers to loans or other debt investments made outside public bond markets, often through a private fund. The strategy, borrowers, terms, and risks vary by investment.

How is private credit different from public bonds?

Private credit investments are typically negotiated and held in private vehicles rather than traded on public bond markets. They may have limited liquidity, less frequent valuations, different fees and reporting, and distinct credit and manager risks.

What should I review before investing in a private credit fund?

Review the strategy, underlying borrowers, manager and underwriting process, leverage, fees, liquidity and redemption terms, valuation practices, capital-call obligations, and how a potential loss or delay would affect your broader plan.

Is private credit appropriate for every high-net-worth investor?

No. Suitability depends on objectives, liquidity needs, time horizon, risk tolerance, existing portfolio, and the terms and eligibility requirements of a specific investment. Private credit can involve loss of principal.

Does private credit provide a guaranteed yield?

No. Income and returns are not guaranteed. Borrowers may default, distributions may vary, and investors may lose some or all of their invested capital.
