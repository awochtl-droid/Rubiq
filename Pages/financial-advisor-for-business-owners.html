A Financial Advisor for Business Owners: What the Work Covers and What It Costs

Most of an owner’s net worth sits inside a company that is hard to value and harder to sell. A financial advisor who works with owners plans around that fact: the retirement plan you design yourself, the tax on what you take out, and the day the business becomes cash.

The short answer

A financial advisor for a business owner plans the owner’s personal finances and the business together. For an employee, planning is mostly about saving and investing. For an owner, the bigger questions are how to get money out of the company efficiently, how to build wealth that does not depend on it, and how to keep more of the proceeds when it is sold.

What a financial advisor does for a business owner

Retirement plan design

You decide which plan the company sponsors, and the choice can be worth six figures a year in deductions. A 401(k) with profit sharing allows up to $75,000 in 2027 before catch-up contributions. A cash balance plan added on top can let an owner in their 50s or 60s deduct several times that. The advisor works with an actuary and your CPA to size the plan, weigh the cost of covering employees, and invest the assets.

Taxes on what you take out and what you sell

Planning covers how you pay yourself, how the entity is taxed, and how a sale will be structured. Whether a deal is an asset sale or a stock sale, how the price is allocated, and whether your shares qualify as qualified small business stock can each move the tax bill by hundreds of thousands of dollars. Our business planning page works through an asset sale versus a stock sale.

Wealth outside the business

A company is a concentrated, illiquid position. The advisor builds a portfolio alongside it that is diversified, accessible, and invested with the business in mind, so a bad year for your industry is not also a bad year for everything else you own. See how we approach investment management.

Protection and continuity

If a partner dies, becomes disabled or wants out, the buy-sell agreement decides what happens, and it only works if it is funded and the valuation in it is current. Key-person and disability coverage protect the business while you are still building it. We review this as part of insurance planning.

Estate planning

A growing business can push an estate toward the federal exemption, which is $15.48 million per person for 2027. Transferring shares to family or trusts before the value rises is far cheaper than doing it after. See the estate tax exemption guide.

Exit and what comes after

The advisor estimates how much you need from a sale, plans the tax with your CPA, and builds the portfolio that replaces your income once the company no longer pays you. Our entrepreneur case study shows how the pieces fit in one transaction.

When to bring an advisor in

  1. While the business is growing. Set up the right retirement plan, separate personal savings from the company, and put buy-sell and key-person coverage in place.
  2. Three to five years before a sale. Get a valuation, clean up the financials, and start the tax planning that needs time, such as large retirement plan contributions and gifts of shares.
  3. When an offer arrives. Model what each deal structure leaves you after tax, and decide how much to take in cash, an earn-out or rolled equity.
  4. After closing. Invest the proceeds, replace your paycheck, and update the estate plan for assets that are now liquid.

Owners most often call at the third step. By then several of the best options are no longer available, which is the main reason to start earlier.

How advisors are paid

Ask this before anything else, because it shapes the advice you get.

  • Fee-only: paid only by advisory or planning fees from clients.
  • Fee-based: paid by advisory fees, and may also earn compensation on some products, most often insurance.
  • Commission-based: paid by the companies whose products are sold.

Rubiq is fee-based. Wealth management is a tiered annual fee that starts at 1.25% of managed assets, with a $500,000 minimum. Planning-only engagements are a flat fee starting at $5,000, with no asset minimum. Rubiq provides advice on insurance products and may earn a fee from placing funds with life insurance companies. That is a conflict of interest we disclose, and you are never obligated to buy insurance through us. The full schedule is on our fee page, and we explain the terms in fee-only vs. fee-based.

Questions to ask before you hire one

  • How many of your clients own a business, and at what stage?
  • Who designs the retirement plan, who invests it, and what does each cost?
  • How do you work with my CPA and attorney, and how often?
  • How are you paid, including on any insurance you recommend?
  • Have you worked with an owner through a sale, and what did you do before and after closing?
  • Are you acting as a fiduciary on everything you recommend?

Independent advisor, private bank or wirehouse

Private banks and wirehouses bring large lending desks and in-house specialists. An independent advisor is not tied to one firm’s products and usually gives you direct access to the person making the decisions. For an owner, the practical test is who will be in the room when the retirement plan is designed and when the letter of intent arrives. Frequently asked questions

What does a financial advisor do for a business owner?

A financial advisor for a business owner connects the company to the owner’s personal finances. The work usually covers retirement plan design, tax planning on compensation and on a future sale, building wealth outside the business, insurance and buy-sell funding, estate planning, and preparing for an exit. The advisor coordinates with the owner’s CPA and attorney and does not replace them.

When should a business owner hire a financial advisor?

The earlier the better, because the most valuable steps take years to pay off. Retirement plan deductions build over time, some sale-tax strategies have multi-year holding periods, and a business is easier to sell after a few years of clean financials. Owners who expect to sell should start at least three to five years ahead.

How much does a financial advisor for business owners cost?

It depends on how the advisor is paid. Fee-only advisors charge only advisory or planning fees. Fee-based advisors charge advisory fees and may also earn compensation on products such as insurance. At Rubiq, wealth management uses a tiered annual fee starting at 1.25% of managed assets with a $500,000 minimum, and planning-only engagements start at a $5,000 flat fee.

Is Rubiq Financial Partners a fee-only advisor?

No. Rubiq is fee-based. Advice and investment management are paid for through advisory fees. Rubiq provides advice on insurance products and may earn a fee from placing funds with life insurance companies, a conflict of interest we disclose. You are never obligated to buy insurance through us.

Does a financial advisor replace my CPA or attorney?

No. Your CPA prepares returns and gives tax advice, and your attorney drafts agreements and estate documents. A financial advisor builds the plan those professionals work from, manages the investments, and keeps the pieces coordinated so that the tax, legal and investment decisions point the same way.

Can a financial advisor help me sell my business?

An advisor does not broker the sale. The advisor’s role is to estimate what you need from the sale, plan for the tax on it with your CPA, coordinate the structure with your attorney and deal team, and invest the proceeds afterward. An investment banker or business broker runs the sale process itself.
