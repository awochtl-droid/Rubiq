Fee-Only vs. Fee-Based Financial Advisors: What’s the Difference?

The difference is whether your advisor can earn anything besides the fees you pay. A fee-only advisor is paid only by clients. A fee-based advisor charges client fees too, but may also earn commissions on certain products. Here is a plain-English breakdown, including where we fit.

By Andreas Wochtl, CFP®, MBA, Founder and Private Wealth Advisor

Fee-only advisors

Fee-only advisors are typically registered investment advisers who hold no brokerage or insurance license. Because they can’t earn commissions, product sales can’t influence their pay.

The tradeoff is access. Some products, like certain insurance policies and some alternative investments, are generally sold on a commission basis. Fee-only clients often have to buy those elsewhere.

Fee-based advisors

Fee-based advisors charge advisory fees for planning and investment management. They may also earn commissions on certain products, such as insurance or, through a broker-dealer, some investments.

Done well, this means one advisor can handle more of your financial life. The responsibility that comes with it is disclosure: you should know in advance when a recommendation would pay the advisor anything beyond your fee.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a fee-only and a fee-based financial advisor?

A fee-only advisor is paid only by clients, through a percentage of assets, a flat fee or an hourly rate. A fee-based advisor also charges client fees but may earn commissions on certain products, such as insurance.

Is a fee-only advisor better than a fee-based advisor?

Not automatically. Both models can serve clients well. What matters is knowing how your advisor is paid, whether they act as a fiduciary, and that every source of compensation is disclosed to you in writing.

Is Rubiq Financial Partners fee-only or fee-based?

Before you hire anyone

Questions to ask any advisor

  1. Are you fee-only, fee-based or commission-based?
  2. Will you earn anything beyond my fee on what you recommend?
  3. Are you acting as a fiduciary on all of my accounts, all the time?
  4. Can I see your Form ADV Part 2 and your Client Relationship Summary (Form CRS)?

A good advisor answers all four without hesitation. We’re happy to walk you through ours.
