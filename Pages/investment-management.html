How we manage your portfolio

  1. Investment policy. We document your goals, time horizon, liquidity needs, risk tolerance, and constraints such as concentrated positions or restricted stock.
  2. Asset allocation. We set long-term targets across stocks, bonds, cash, and, where appropriate, alternative investments.
  3. Asset location. We decide which holdings belong in taxable, IRA, and Roth accounts to reduce annual tax drag.
  4. Implementation. We choose low-cost index funds, separately managed accounts, or direct indexing based on account size and tax situation.
  5. Rebalancing within bands. We rebalance when allocations drift beyond set ranges, using cash flows and tax-deferred accounts first. See tax-aware rebalancing.
  6. Review and reporting. We report performance on a consolidated, after-tax-aware basis and revisit the plan as your life changes.

Assets are held at an independent custodian, primarily Charles Schwab. See our fee structure for how we are paid.

A worked example: the same portfolio, $17,905 less tax

A Pennsylvania couple in the top bracket has $1,000,000 in IRAs and $1,000,000 in a taxable account, and wants half in bonds yielding 5% and half in a stock index fund yielding 1.5% in qualified dividends. The overall allocation is identical in both versions; only the location changes.

Bonds in taxable, stocks in IRAStocks in taxable, bonds in IRA
Taxable income from the taxable account$50,000 of bond interest$15,000 of qualified dividends
Tax rate on that income43.87% (37% + 3.8% + 3.07% PA)26.87% (20% + 3.8% + 3.07% PA)
Annual tax$21,935$4,031

Putting the bonds inside the IRA cuts this year’s tax by about $17,905, and the stocks in the taxable account keep the option of tax-loss harvesting and a step-up in basis for heirs. Asset location is not always this clean; Roth accounts, required distributions, and charitable plans all change the answer. Our asset location guide goes further. Frequently asked questions

What is tax-efficient investment management?

Tax-efficient investment management minimizes the tax drag on portfolio returns through strategies like tax-loss harvesting, asset location optimization, and gain deferral. For UHNW investors, this can mean the difference between keeping 60% or 80% of gross returns over a 20-year horizon.

What is direct indexing and how does it reduce taxes?

Direct indexing involves owning individual securities that replicate an index rather than buying the index fund itself. This allows advisors to harvest losses on individual positions while maintaining market exposure — generating tax deductions that can offset gains elsewhere in the portfolio.

Should I use separately managed accounts or mutual funds?

For taxable accounts above $500K, separately managed accounts (SMAs) typically offer superior tax efficiency because the advisor can harvest losses on individual positions. Mutual funds distribute capital gains to all shareholders regardless of when you bought in, creating unwanted tax events.

How does Rubiq coordinate investment management with tax planning?

We manage investments and tax strategy as a single, integrated process. Before rebalancing, harvesting, or making any portfolio change, we model the tax impact against your full return, your bracket, and your estate plan. This coordination is what separates fiduciary wealth management from simple asset allocation.

What is asset location?

Asset location is deciding which investments to hold in taxable, tax-deferred, and tax-free accounts. Placing income-heavy assets such as taxable bonds in IRAs, and tax-efficient assets such as broad stock index funds in taxable accounts, can reduce annual taxes without changing the overall allocation.

Where are my assets held?

Client assets are held at an independent third-party custodian, primarily Charles Schwab, not by Rubiq. You retain direct access to your accounts and receive statements from the custodian.
