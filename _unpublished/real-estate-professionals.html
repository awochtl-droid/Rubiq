Where agents tend to get asked for help

Sellers rarely ask their agent about taxes, but they ask about what to do next. Most of the time that question is about the gain: how much of it is taxable, whether to exchange into another property, and what else is available if they are finished managing tenants.

We take that handoff after you have done your job. The client keeps their own CPA and attorney. We work alongside them and the qualified intermediary, and we cover the investment side of the decision.

  • DSTs and 1031 exchanges: how a Delaware Statutory Trust can serve as replacement property when a client wants income without management.
  • DST vs. TIC vs. REIT: how the options compare on control, liquidity and taxes.
  • Depreciation recapture: why the tax bill on a rental sale is often bigger than the seller expects.
  • 721 exchanges: what happens when a DST later rolls into a REIT.
  • Step-up in basis: when holding the property is the better plan.

What happens when you introduce a client

Earlier is better. A client who calls before listing has more choices than one who calls after closing, because the exchange deadlines start at closing.

  1. A short introductory call

    Andreas hears the situation: what they own, what they paid, what they want next.

  2. We work with their other advisors

    Their CPA confirms the tax picture and their attorney handles the transaction. We coordinate with both, and with the qualified intermediary if an exchange is on the table.

  3. A written plan before any decision

    You see the options side by side, including the one that does nothing, with the tax and liquidity trade-offs spelled out.

  4. You stay in the loop if the client wants you to

    Clients decide whether we keep you updated. Either way, the relationship is theirs.
